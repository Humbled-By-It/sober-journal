Sobriety is not a straight line. A hard day never erases the clean ones behind it.

Hi, I'm Beck & this is my 2026 journey to sobriety from weed.

I know people say you can't get addicted to it, but they're wrong. I'm a burnout millennial with two graduate degrees who has lived and worked in Central America, Asia, and Eastern Europe. I've used cannabis as a coping mechanism and treatment for undiagnosed ADHD since my early twenties. I was finally diagnosed at 31, and the treatment and specified therapy I've received for that has changed my life to the point where I could finally address my addiction to that glorious flower, Mary Jane.

I chose to count collective sober days rather than a single "quit date" because the idea of actually quitting terrified me, and I've felt like a failure every time I've tried to quit and relapsed. Fun fact: more than half of people who try to quit an addiction relapse. It's part of the sobriety journey, not the end of it. Letting go of all that all-or-nothing thinking is what helped me start walking this path in the first place, and it's what's helped me stick with it.

If you want to start your own sobriety journal, for whatever your addiction may be, you're welcome to use mine.

  1. Download the starter template from my GitHub repo (a file called starter-template.html). It's the same tool you're looking at now, stripped of my entries and ready for yours.
  2. Try it right away by just opening the file in your browser — it runs on your own computer, no account needed. Name what you're stepping back from, pick a start date, and check in.
  3. Want it online like mine? Make a free account at github.com, create a new public repository, upload the file, and rename it index.html.
  4. In the repo's Settings → Pages, set the source to "Deploy from a branch" (main / root). In a minute or two, GitHub gives you your own free public link.

Your entries live in your own browser, private to you, until you choose to share them the way I've shared mine.

Or you can simply enjoy reading my story below, provided to you, dear internet strangers, in raw, unedited journal entries. I hope something in my ramblings sticks with you and helps you on your own path.
